Single-stage planetary gearbox

As part of the FSAE capstone (MQP), I co-designed a nitrided-gear, steel-housing planetary gearbox for a dual rear-wheel EMRAX 308 motor layout. It is built to be machined entirely in-house, which removes outsourcing cost and lead time. Two units are in production.

Parametric sprocket, topology optimized

A 36-tooth, 520-pitch rear sprocket for the rebuilt car, modeled in SolidWorks with equation-driven tooth geometry. The 200 mm stock limits it to 36 teeth, so the final drive ratio is 2.77 instead of the ideal 4.15. I accepted that trade-off rather than shrink the chain pitch, which would weaken the chain.

I ran four topology studies, then FEA with the chain load spread across the 15 teeth in contact. The final design cut mass from 444 g to 262 g (40.96%) with a peak stress of 216.6 MPa against a 505 MPa yield strength, a safety factor of 2.33 against a 2.0 target. I milled it from 7075-T6 aluminum and cut the internal spline on the wire EDM.

2018–19 legacy vehicle rebuild

I started this rebuild on my own to bring the team’s retired 2018–19 car, powered by a YZ450F, back to running condition with better corner-exit traction and power delivery. I installed a Drexler limited-slip differential and tuned the engine.

I also designed and FEA-validated the 6061 aluminum differential mounts. They are adapted from an existing design for the same differential, realigned to the drive and driven sprockets, with SKF bearings on each side and turnbuckle attachments for chain tensioning.
